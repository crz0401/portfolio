Program Design: Turning Objectives into a Plan

I revised the learning schedule, industry and business content, and team activities, then organized them into a practical plan with distinct learning phases.

A

Three Design Changes

The findings shaped three changes to what participants learned and how the program was run.

01

Adjust the Learning Pace

Spread learning across several phases

Previous approach and limitations

Previously, role-specific sessions were packed into the in-person training period. The schedule was dense, participants had a lot to absorb and teams had to allow more time away from work.

Changes for this cohort

Company introductory courses moved to the preparatory phase. Repeated sessions offered more choice, with in-person and online attendance available at the same time. Role-specific sessions were scheduled after the in-person training period.

The revised schedule supported all three objectives, giving participants more time to absorb the material while fitting around project work.

02

Broaden Industry and Business Understanding

Build a fuller view of the industry and business

Learning objective

Help new hires understand the industry and the company’s business through shared learning.

Changes for this cohort

I redesigned sessions on SLG industry trends, AI in game development and publishing, and game development and operations workflows. Feedback from new hires and project teams also shaped the role-specific courses.

03

Strengthen Collaborative Learning

Develop workplace skills through shared tasks

Learning objective

Give new hires opportunities to work together, practice collaboration and build peer connections.

Changes for this cohort

I retained the GameJam and added team development activities. Group debriefs and team presentations further deepened participants’ understanding of workplace collaboration and connected the activities to specific work expectations.

Case 01

Ad Creative Production Session

Turning expert experience into methods new hires could use

The session helped new hires in ad creative production understand their responsibilities and helped colleagues in other roles understand the production process and how to work with the creative team. Drawing on feedback from new hires and project teams, I worked with the instructor to set the focus, suggest examples and review the content.

  1. 01
    Review learning needs

    Summarized feedback from new hires and departments

  2. 02
    Work with the instructor

    Discussed challenges the instructor had seen while mentoring new hires and how they addressed them

  3. 03
    Plan the structure and examples

    Agreed on the outline and suggested using cases to explain the work

  4. 04
    Review and refine

    Reviewed the content and suggested revisions

One recommendation from my content review

I suggested comparing two scenarios using ROI, impressions and spend: one where all three indicators showed good performance, and one where a single indicator underperformed. This would help new hires use market data to assess ad creative performance and decide what to improve.

Final course structure

  1. Understand the Role — Responsibilities and value
  2. Build a Broader Perspective — Creative production and performance analysis workflows
  3. Build Skills — Core skills and development paths
  4. Address Challenges — Common challenges for new hires and AI applications

Tangram Team Simulation

Seven groups worked together on different tasks with limited resources and restrictions on information sharing. I helped design the activity, hosted it, guided the debriefs and delivered the closing summary. I linked the introduction to the previous day’s session on game development and operations, so participants could explore cross-functional collaboration through the task.

05

Personal Reflection

A

Define What the Program Should Address Before Choosing Content

Stakeholder needs must be prioritized to form clear learning objectives. These priorities determine what to include, how deeply to cover it and how to allocate time and resources. Shared training, role-specific courses and ongoing mentoring serve different purposes. What each should address—and where its responsibility ends—should be clear before content is selected.

B

Review Content for Its Usefulness in Practice

A course can cover a topic thoroughly and still be hard for new hires to use. Course development and activity debriefs should turn expert experience into clear examples, ways to assess situations and practical actions. Content review should ask both whether the explanation is clear and whether a new hire could use it to make a decision and act.

C

Use Feedback from Everyday Work to Improve the Program

What the Current Evaluation Covers

The evaluation covered participant experience, understanding of the content and team results. It told us less about whether new hires continued to use what they learned in everyday work.

Next Step

Choose a few workplace behaviors to observe and agree with mentors on what to look for and when to follow up. Use their feedback on how new hires apply the learning to decide which content to keep and which to revise.
